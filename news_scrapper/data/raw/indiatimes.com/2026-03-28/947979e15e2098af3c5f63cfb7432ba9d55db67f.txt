Chinese banking stocks showed relative resilience in early Friday trade, outperforming the broader market after a report suggested regulators may relax shareholder restrictions to support capital raising efforts. According to Reuters, China’s banking regulator, the National Financial Regulatory Administration , is considering allowing certain shareholders to expand their presence across more lenders. The proposal would permit some investors to become major shareholders, defined as holding stakes of 5% or more, in up to two additional banks beyond the current cap of two. The potential policy shift is seen as a move to provide banks with greater flexibility in raising capital, either through private placements or public share issuance. The regulator did not respond to a request for comment on the proposed changes. Market reaction reflected cautious optimism. The benchmark CSI300 index opened about 1% lower before trimming losses, while the CSI Banks Index showed relative strength, opening marginally lower and trading near flat in early hours. Analysts believe the proposed easing could have a meaningful impact on the sector. Citi noted that such a move would likely support Chinese banks by accelerating loan growth, improving management incentives to enhance profitability, and attracting incremental institutional investment. JPMorgan echoed similar views, stating that loosening ownership restrictions could broaden the investor base for Chinese lenders and provide a positive tailwind for the sector. The development could also benefit large financial conglomerates such as Ping An Insurance Group of China. Ping An, which already holds a controlling stake in Ping An Bank , is currently restricted from becoming a major shareholder in additional lenders under existing rules. A relaxation could enable the group to expand its holdings beyond its current limits. JPMorgan highlighted that banks within Ping An’s investment portfolio, including Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, China Construction Bank, Postal Savings Bank of China, and China Merchants Bank, could emerge as key beneficiaries if the rules are eased, Reuters said. Overall, the potential regulatory shift signals Beijing’s intent to strengthen the banking system’s capital base and stimulate investor participation, offering a supportive backdrop for the sector despite broader market weakness.
