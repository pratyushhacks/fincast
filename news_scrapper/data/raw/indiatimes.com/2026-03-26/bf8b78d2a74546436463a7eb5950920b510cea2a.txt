Global brokerage Barclays has struck a cautiously optimistic tone on U.S. equities, raising its year-end 2026 target for the S&P 500 even as geopolitical tensions, inflation concerns and financial system stress continue to cloud the broader outlook, as per a report by Reuters. The firm now expects the benchmark index to reach 7,650 by the end of 2026, up from its earlier projection of 7,400. Based on recent levels, the revised target suggests a potential upside of over 16%, reflecting confidence in the durability of corporate earnings and the resilience of the U.S. economy. Earnings Strength Drives Optimism At the core of Barclays’ upgraded outlook is the expectation that earnings growth, particularly led by technology companies, will remain robust. The brokerage has raised its earnings per share (EPS) estimate for the S&P 500 to $321 for 2026, up from $305 earlier, indicating that the anticipated market gains are rooted in fundamental performance rather than a re-rating of valuations. According to the Reuters report, Barclays believes the U.S. continues to stand out among major economies due to stronger nominal growth and a structural advantage in technology-driven expansion. This long-term growth engine, supported by ongoing investments in artificial intelligence and digital infrastructure, is expected to underpin corporate profitability. Geopolitical Tensions Keep Markets on Edge Despite the constructive outlook, markets have been unsettled in the near term. The S&P 500 has declined around 4% since the onset of the Iran conflict , as investors reacted to rising oil prices and escalating geopolitical uncertainty by shifting toward safer assets. The surge in crude prices has reignited concerns around inflation, complicating the policy path for the U.S. Federal Reserve. With inflation risks resurfacing, the central bank has signaled a more cautious stance, indicating limited room for rate cuts in the coming year. Macro Risks Could Trigger Volatility Barclays has acknowledged that the road ahead may not be smooth. The brokerage outlined a downside scenario where the S&P 500 could fall to 5,900 if adverse conditions persist. A key risk identified is the possibility of sustained high oil prices feeding into inflation, which could constrain the Federal Reserve’s ability to support growth. The report also highlighted Barclays’ concerns around emerging stress in private credit markets. Rising redemption pressures in these funds could amplify market volatility, especially if investor sentiment weakens further. Additionally, the increasing pace of disruption from artificial intelligence remains a double-edged sword; while it supports long-term growth, it may also create short-term dislocations across industries. Sectoral Shifts Reflect Changing Dynamics In line with its evolving macro view, Barclays has made selective changes to its sector preferences. Industrials have been upgraded to a positive stance, supported by improving momentum and increased capital expenditure tied to AI and infrastructure. Meanwhile, materials and energy sectors have been raised to a neutral outlook, reflecting the benefits of higher commodity prices and strengthening demand conditions. These adjustments suggest a broader shift toward cyclical and real-economy sectors that could benefit from ongoing investment cycles and pricing power. A Bullish View, With Caution While Barclays remains incrementally bullish on U.S. equities, the firm acknowledges that volatility is likely to persist in the near term. The interplay between strong earnings growth and mounting macroeconomic risks will be crucial in determining market direction. The brokerage expects markets to remain choppy until greater clarity emerges on geopolitical developments, inflation trends and monetary policy.
