NEW DELHI: As artificial intelligence accelerates data consumption and real-time application demands, telecom networks must evolve from capacity-driven architectures to intelligent, AI-native systems, top private sector executives and a government official said on Friday. They emphasised that scaling 5G and cloud infrastructure will depend on embedding AI across network layers, enabling automation, efficiency, and new revenue opportunities. The discussion took place at the ETTelecom 5G Congress 2026 , during a panel featuring Anil Kumar Bhardwaj, DDG (SE), Department of Telecommunications, Gulshan Khurana, EVP, Vodafone Idea (Vi), Munish Aggarwal, director, Ciena , and Umang Jindal, head of networks & performance, Southeast Asia, Oceania and India, Ericsson . Bhardwaj underscored the importance of enabling innovation while maintaining a level playing field for operators. He noted that while the government provides flexibility in technology and pricing decisions, the onus of building sustainable business models lies with the industry. “We give you the freedom to decide your technology and pricing—operators must now find viable monetisation models,” he said, pointing to the growing challenge of revenue generation in an AI-driven ecosystem. Khurana highlighted how AI is being integrated across the network—from edge to core—to handle evolving traffic patterns and performance requirements. He stressed the shift from throughput-centric metrics to intelligence-driven performance. “The journey is moving from Gbps to smart, AI-enabled networks that can deliver real-time analytics and efficiency." Infrastructure providers echoed the need for scalable and efficient architectures. Aggarwal pointed out that AI workloads are driving demand for high-capacity, low-latency networks, while also requiring optimisation of cost and energy consumption. “When we build scalable networks for AI, we not only increase capacity but also reduce cost and energy per bit,” he said, highlighting the importance of sustainable network design. In turn, Jindal emphasised the convergence of network, compute, and cloud, noting that traditional boundaries are disappearing. He highlighted the rise of distributed computing and edge intelligence as key enablers of future networks. “There are no silos anymore—compute, network, and applications are coming together to deliver performance on demand,” Jindal said. The panel also discussed the operational impact of AI, particularly in predictive maintenance , automated optimisation, and intent-based networking. AI is enabling networks to dynamically adapt to changing traffic conditions, improving quality of service and reducing manual intervention. At the same time, the top executives flagged monetisation as a critical challenge. While AI and 5G unlock new capabilities, operators must identify viable revenue streams—particularly in enterprise segments such as SMEs, private networks, and industry-specific use cases. The session was moderated by Tarun Pathak, research director at Counterpoint Research. He highlighted the dual shift underway in the industry—rapid 5G rollout alongside exponential growth in AI-led applications. This, he noted, is fundamentally changing network design priorities. “We are moving towards networks that are more dynamic, distributed, and AI-driven,” Pathak said, framing the need for next-generation architectures. The discussion concluded with a consensus that India’s scale and evolving digital ecosystem present a unique opportunity to lead in AI-driven telecom innovation. However, realising this potential will require stronger collaboration across policy, infrastructure, and application layers to build sustainable and monetisable network models.
