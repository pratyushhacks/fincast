While Gujarat Government maintains that there is enough provision of LPG in the state, the migrant labourers in Surat’s textile industry are now steadily living the state due to LPG shortage . Speaking to ET from Surat, Ashish Gujarati, former president of the South Gujarat Chamber of Commerce admitted that while the shortage of gas has hit the industry at the level of production itself, the migrant workers are also now leaving the state as they are in no position to procure cooking gas cylinders for themselves. ALSO READ: West Asia crisis hits smartphone supply, India shipment outlook cut again “The war has hit us at several fronts,” Gujarati said adding that while on one hand the cost of production has sky rocketed with the enhanced cost of synthetic yarn and limited supply of gas largely by Gujarat Gas, the migrant labourers are now steadily leaving the state due to unavailability of cooking gas. Surat for the records is home to about 15 lakh migrant textile workers. “We are arranging some community kitchens for the workers in some places, but given the large number of workers in the city, such efforts are hardly effective to contain the situation,” Gujarati added. Nirav Rana, a trade unionist and President of South Gujarat Productivity Council told ET that while approximately 5 to 6 lakh people have already left the state, more are on their way. “Primarily LPG connections are issued against ration cards and these migrant workers don’t have ration cards from here,” Rana said adding that while most of the migrant workers normally source their cooking gas supply from the open market, the ongoing situation has absolutely halted the supply for them. Rana went on to say that while the weaving sector has already taken a major hit due to the crisis, several units are now running four days a week. “If this continues, the owners will find it difficult to keep the units running at all soon,” he added.
