Six top Meta executives could receive bonuses close to $1 billion each as the company pushes big plans in artificial intelligence. This news came even as Meta laid off about 700 employees on Wednesday. CEO Mark Zuckerberg introduced a new stock option plan that could give executives up to $921 million each. The executives most likely to get the biggest payouts include Chief Technology Officer Andrew Bosworth, Chief Product Officer Chris Cox, and Chief Operating Officer Javier Olivan, according to Equilar, a compensation research firm as stated by Daily Mail. The smallest estimated bonus would go to Chief Financial Officer Susan Li, at around $161 million. Meta stock options plan President and vice chairman Dina Powell McCormick and Chief Legal Officer Chris Mahoney would also qualify for stock options. This would be the first time since 2012 that Meta has offered stock options to executives. A Meta spokesperson said the payouts depend on huge company success and would benefit shareholders too, according to The New York Times. Executives may also be allowed to buy more stock options if Meta hits certain growth goals. Meta $9 trillion target One big target is making Meta a $9 trillion company by 2031, which would mean increasing its value about six times. Zuckerberg is focused on building “superintelligence” and Meta is expected to spend about $115 billion this year, mostly on AI. Zuckerberg, whose net worth is $204.2 billion, said AI will dramatically change how people work starting this year. He also warned that AI improvements could lead to layoffs. AI layoffs and hiring Zuckerberg told investors that projects once needing large teams can now be done by one very talented person, according to The New York Times. Meta spent billions last year hiring AI specialists to join its workforce of around 78,000 employees, as cited by Daily Mail. Hundreds of workers were cut this week from Reality Labs, the team focused on virtual reality and metaverse products. Meta lawsuit social media addiction The layoffs represented about 10 to 15 percent of that team. A Meta spokesperson said restructuring happens regularly to help teams meet goals. The news also comes after Meta and Google were ordered to pay $3 million to a woman who said social media addiction harmed her life. The 20-year-old plaintiff, called Kaley, claimed the platforms hooked her when she was young. After nine days of deliberations, California jurors ruled both companies were negligent in designing or running their platforms. Jurors said the companies’ actions contributed to Kaley’s mental health struggles and addiction. The jury also found the platforms knew their services could be dangerous to minors but failed to warn users. Meta was assigned 70 percent of responsibility, equal to $2.1 million of the award, as noted by Daily Mail. YouTube was assigned the remaining 30 percent, or $900,000. FAQs Q1.
