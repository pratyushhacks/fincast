Sonepat, Haryana The persistent gap between what engineering colleges teach and what technology companies need has frustrated employers and graduates for decades. While institutions focus on theoretical foundations, industries evolve rapidly, creating mismatches that leave graduates underprepared for actual workplace demands. SBIT in Delhi NCR works with Amazon Web Services to help bridge this gap through curriculum guidance, faculty development support, and pathways connecting student learning to employment opportunities in cloud computing and related technology fields. The Education-to-Employment Gap Technology industry hiring managers consistently report difficulties finding adequately prepared engineering graduates. Many graduates possess theoretical knowledge but lack practical skills making them immediately productive in technology roles. For cloud computing specifically, the gap proves particularly pronounced. Cloud platforms have transformed how software systems are built, deployed, and scaled, yet many engineering programs still teach computing primarily through traditional approaches. Graduates enter workplaces where cloud-first approaches dominate but lack hands-on experience with major platforms. Employers respond by creating extensive onboarding programs, essentially teaching graduates skills they could have learned during undergraduate education. Educational institutions struggle to close these gaps independently—faculty may lack current industry experience, accessing enterprise-grade cloud infrastructure requires investment, and curriculum processes move slowly while technology evolves rapidly. What AWS Collaboration Provides AWS collaboration operates through structured arrangements providing guidance on modernizing education programs, leadership development for institutional administrators, faculty development to enhance teaching capabilities, employer connections, and student career support to improve employment outcomes. AWS does not deliver courses directly to students. This distinguishes the collaboration from simple training programs. AWS provides guidance, resources, and industry perspective to help institutions teach more effectively. Programs provide participating institutions access to educational materials and resources supporting cloud education. Institutions can integrate these materials into courses. Faculty development represents a crucial component, helping engineering professors gain current industry experience with cloud platforms and practices. Employer connections provide intelligence about what skills technology companies need, enabling curriculum adjustments. Student career support creates pathways from education to industry careers rather than students graduating with skills but no clear path to using them professionally. Educational Program Arrangements SBIT has arrangements with AWS establishing certain responsibilities and boundaries. Under such arrangements, institutions typically confirm that participation complies with applicable laws, regulations, and internal requirements. Institutions determine and obtain necessary approvals and regulatory clearances required to participate and utilize program materials in teaching. Restrictions often govern public discussion of such arrangements. Institutions may need approval before communicating publicly about program participation. Any publicity may require review and approval of specific content. Institutions typically cannot rebrand educational programs to suggest AWS involvement in granting degrees, diplomas, or certificates. Programs cannot include AWS references in degree credentials. Curriculum materials prepared under programs typically cannot be rebranded suggesting AWS created them. These restrictions reflect realities about educational credentialing. Degrees carry legal and regulatory significance. Suggesting AWS involvement in granting academic credentials could create compliance issues or mislead students. Programs enhance education but don’t replace institutional responsibility for academic standards. Student Privacy Considerations AWS collaboration arrangements typically include data privacy protections. Institutions generally do not share personal data from students or employees with AWS under educational programs. This represents a boundary protecting student privacy. Partnerships involve curriculum guidance, faculty development, and career pathways—not student data collection or analysis. This prevents potential concerns. AWS cannot use student information for marketing, cannot analyze student performance data to improve commercial products, and cannot create profiles tracking students beyond educational context. The learning relationship remains between students and institutions. For students and parents evaluating AWS partnerships, understanding data privacy protections proves important. Such arrangements can enhance educational quality and career opportunities without creating privacy risks or data sharing obligations. Academic Independence Arrangements typically establish that parties do not intend to jointly develop or create intellectual property. Institutions receive limited licensing rights to AWS materials but cannot modify them substantially, create derivative works, or sublicense them to others. Importantly, parties typically operate as independent entities. Neither party can bind the other to agreements or make obligations on the other’s behalf. This preserves institutional independence in making educational decisions while enabling structured collaboration. Provisions prove particularly important for academic independence. AWS cannot be referenced in degrees or diplomas. Institutions must make their own assessments about curriculum appropriateness and compliance with educational regulations. These boundaries ensure that while AWS offers industry perspective and resources, institutions maintain control over academic decisions, degree standards, and educational integrity.
