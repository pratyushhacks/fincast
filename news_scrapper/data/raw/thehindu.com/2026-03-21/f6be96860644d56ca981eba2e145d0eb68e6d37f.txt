The Indian stock markets experienced a meltdown on Thursday, crashing over 3% , as oil price spiked to $114 a barrel and the U.S. Federal Reserve signalled higher inflation. Gold prices also tumbled 3% on fears of inflation, while the rupee fell to a new low. “Stock market news can usually be found on our business pages, but such a significant crash merited space on our front page today, especially since it was triggered by oil prices rising to record rates on the back of the war in West Asia,” says Suresh Nambath, Editor, The Hindu. “In our editorial today , we point out that this ill-conceived, illegal war has brought death and destruction to West Asia, and economic havoc on the whole world, India included.” Along with Iran’s closure of the Gulf of Hormuz, strikes by both Israel and Iran on major oil and gas facilities have spooked the markets. It spurred U.S. President Donald Trump to try to distance himself from the Israeli strike on the South Pars gas field, though Israeli officials told American media outlets that the U.S. had been informed of the attack. The markets witnessed the perfect storm as Iran retaliated by attacking major energy sites in the Gulf region. This was the worst day on Dalal Street since June 2024, when the markets crashed over 5% in a single session. This is also only the fifth time since 2021 that benchmark indices have dipped lower than 3%. The Nifty opened at 23,197.75 and Sensex at 74,750.92 points, down about 2.4% from the previous close. Both the indices maintained the level before crashing to the day’s low of 22,930.35 and 73,950.95 points before closing at 23,002.15 and 74,207.24 points. These were levels that markets were trading at in mid-2024. All 21 sectoral indices were down with Nifty Auto down more than 4%. The sudden slump towards the final trading hours was due to soaring Brent crude futures price hitting a new high of $114 a barrel and the rupee depreciating to a new low of ₹92.89 a dollar. Increasing risk of inflation due to the war in West Asia led gold tumbling 3% to $4,650 an ounce. “The decline was largely driven by the Federal Reserve’s hawkish stance, with any potential easing now contingent on clear signs of moderating inflation,” said Kaynat Chainwala, AVP Commodity Research, Kotak Securities. On the global macro front, the U.S. central bank – the Federal Reserve – held interest rates steady ranging between 3.5% and 3.75%, signalling higher inflation could stymie further rate cuts in 2026. But a rate hike by the U.S. Fed would make American markets more attractive for foreign funds, intensifying their current exit from the Indian market.
