Backed by strong cash flows from its lead-acid business, Exide is positioning itself in India’s fast-growing EV battery market, supported by strategic partnerships with Hyundai and Kia and ongoing talks with domestic EV manufacturers. Exide Industries on Wednesday said in a regulatory filing that it has infused another ₹450 crore into its electric vehicle battery subsidiary, Exide Energy Solutions Ltd (EESL), as its lithium-ion gigafactory in Bengaluru enters the final leg of commissioning, marking a shift from build-out to execution. The investment comes via the allotment of 11.25 crore equity shares at ₹40 apiece on a rights basis, taking Exide’s total commitment to ₹4,802 crore while retaining full ownership. Gigafactory nears commissioning, production timeline set The funds are earmarked for last-mile requirements, including equipment calibration, trial runs and working capital, with the company targeting commercial production in the first half of FY26. Phase 1 of the facility will have 6 GWh of annual capacity, spanning NMC cells for high-performance EVs and LFP chemistry for mass-market vehicles and stationary storage. The financial trade-off, however, is immediate. EESL reported a loss of ₹209 crore in FY25, and the ramp-up phase is expected to weigh on margins due to high depreciation and operating costs. “The next 12–18 months will be a drag on consolidated profitability, but that’s typical for a project of this scale. Break-even is likely only by late FY27 or early FY28 once utilisation crosses 40–50%,” added an analyst at a leading brokerage. Core business supports investment cycle Company insiders, however, maintain that the balance sheet can absorb the transition. “The lead-acid business continues to generate strong cash flows, with steady 7–9% growth led by replacement demand and OEM traction. That gives us the ability to invest through the cycle,” a senior executive said. Management has indicated that the lithium-ion business will begin contributing meaningfully to revenues by FY27, with the group targeting around ₹20,000 crore in revenue by FY28 and ₹25,000 crore by 2030. Strategic tie-ups ensure demand visibility Crucially, Exide is not entering the market without visibility on demand. In 2024, it signed a strategic MoU with Hyundai Motor and Kia Corp to supply locally manufactured LFP cells for upcoming EV models in India, providing a ready offtake pipeline. The company is also in advanced talks with domestic electric two-wheeler makers, targeting the e-scooter segment that remains dependent on imports. Competition intensifies in battery space This positions Exide distinctly in an intensifying battery race. Amara Raja is scaling up its own gigafactory with a 2027 timeline, while Ola Electric has already operationalised in-house cells. “Exide’s advantage is that it is entering commissioning with a visible order book, reducing the risk of idle capacity—one of the biggest concerns in this space,” the analyst added.
