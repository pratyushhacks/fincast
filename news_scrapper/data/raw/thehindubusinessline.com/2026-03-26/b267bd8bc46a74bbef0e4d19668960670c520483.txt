• By Ravneet Pawha, VP (Global Engagement) and CEO (South Asia), Deakin University India’s economic ascent is no longer a projection; it is a measurable reality. Nowhere is this more visible than in financial services. India’s Banking, Financial Services and Insurance (BFSI) sector contributed ₹166.5 trillion to the national economy in 2024. It stands at the very core of India’s growth engine. And yet, beneath this strength lies a paradox. India’s financial sector is expanding rapidly, but the talent pipeline required to sustain that growth is under strain. Frontline attrition in BFSI stands at an extraordinary 103% annually, meaning most frontline roles are refilled more than once a year. Operational gaps exist in banks and insurers at precisely the moment when agility is critical. The message is clear: infrastructure and capital are scaling faster than capability. As GIFT City positions itself as India’s gateway to global finance, this challenge becomes even more consequential. Beyond regulation or tax efficiency, a financial capital is defined by the quality, resilience and readiness of its talent. India today hosts more than 190 BFSI Global Capability Centres (GCCs), employing approximately 540,000 professionals and the sector is projected to grow from roughly USD 40 billion in 2023 to as much as USD 125–135 billion by 2032, expanding at a 12–13% CAGR. Global confidence in India’s financial and digital ecosystem is unmistakable. Growth at this scale also brings an opportunity to strengthen and deepen capability across the ecosystem. There is currently a 42% skill gap in AI and data roles within BFSI GCCs. As institutions embed machine learning, robotic process automation and advanced analytics into core processes, demand for specialised talent is outpacing supply. Salaries are rising, competition is intensifying, and hiring cycles are elongating. This is not simply a recruitment challenge. It is a structural transformation. The Indian finance workforce is undergoing an AI-driven shift. Traditional, rule-based tasks are being replaced by AI-enabled risk analysis, automated onboarding and KYC systems, NLP-driven advisory platforms and predictive compliance monitoring. Fintechs, NBFCs and banks are redesigning core operations around data. Data literacy, algorithmic thinking and AI governance are no longer niche competencies; they are foundational skills. Similarly, the industry’s expectations of professional profiles are evolving. A majority of employers now prefer candidates with a combination of advanced management education and globally benchmarked finance credentials. Market and product knowledge, financial modelling, stakeholder communication and regulatory interpretation are increasingly valued in tandem. The finance professional of the future must be analytically sharp, technologically fluent and ethically grounded. Emerging roles underscore this shift. Cyber fraud analysts, Cloud FinOps professionals, sustainability specialists aligned to ESG-linked finance, and platform-oriented risk architects are shaping a new employment landscape. Even specialised roles intersecting with energy finance and digital infrastructure are gaining prominence as capital flows diversify. Importantly, this opportunity is no longer confined to metropolitan centres. Tier-II cities such as Gandhinagar, Jaipur and Kochi are emerging as credible hiring hubs for BFSI GCC expansion. India’s talent map is broadening, but scale must be matched with quality. ‘We are at a juncture where education must evolve with urgency.’ Industry-integrated academic design is not a luxury; it is a necessity. Curriculum must reflect AI-led transformation. Students must engage with live regulatory scenarios. Risk modelling must incorporate real-world datasets. Compliance must be taught not as theory, but as practice. Industry immersion must be embedded from the outset. Global standards matter here. In a financial capital aspiring to international stature, professionals must be trained to operate within global regulatory frameworks, not only domestic ones. Ethical reasoning must accompany technical mastery. Governance must be internalised, not memorised. The graduation of the inaugural cohort from Deakin University’s GIFT City campus, therefore, carries significance beyond the ceremony. Our graduates enter the workforce at a pivotal moment, as India’s BFSI ecosystem is expanding rapidly yet demanding deeper capabilities. They represent the first wave of globally benchmarked finance professionals trained within India’s newest international financial ecosystem. They have been shaped in an environment aligned to global academic standards, industry integration and regulatory awareness. Their role is not merely to fill vacancies. It is to strengthen the capability architecture of India’s financial future. India is poised to become a financial capital of consequence. The expansion of GCCs, the AI-led transformation of banking, the rise of fintech, and the decentralisation of talent hubs all point to momentum. ‘The current economic momentum must be sustained by competence.’ A financial capital of the future demands future-ready talent, professionals who understand AI as deeply as accounting, governance as seriously as growth, and global standards as naturally as local context. Growth at this scale creates a powerful opportunity to build deeper expertise and elevate capability across the entire ecosystem.
