The rupee has recovered slightly from its low and has closed at 93.87 on Tuesday. The Indian rupee has been knocked down badly in the past week. The domestic currency tumbled to a new low of 93.98 against the dollar. Crude oil price spiking back to $119 per barrel last week coupled with strong foreign money outflows dragged the rupee lower. The Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) have been on a selling spree since the beginning of this month. The Indian market has seen a net outflow of about $12.5 billion so far this month. The rupee has recovered slightly from its low and has closed at 93.87 on Tuesday. Rupee outlook There is no major support visible on the charts. Resistance is at 93.20 and then in the 93-92.90 region. If the rupee manages to sustain above 94, there are good chances to see some recovery towards 93.20-93 this week. This recovery is also possible if the Centre intervenes to limit the downside in the domestic currency. However, from a big picture, as long as the rupee stays below 92.90, the bias will remain negative. There is a danger of the rupee falling to 95.30 in the coming months. A strong break below 94 can trigger this fall. Rupee will now have to rise above 93 to get some breather. Only then a strong recovery towards 92 is possible.  Such a move is possible only if the central bank intervenes. In the absence of that, a fall to 95.30 cannot be ruled out. Strong dollar The dollar index (99.33) has come down below 100 last week. However, there is a strong support  in the 99-98.80 region which can be tested in the near term. But thereafter, the dollar index can rise back above 100 and go up to 101 initially. From a medium-term perspective, the dollar index has potential to see 103-104 on the upside. A strong dollar can keep the rupee under pressure. Volatile oil Crude oil price has been volatile. We can expect the Brent Crude Oil ($103 per barrel) to oscillate in a wide range of $90-$120 per barrel for some time now. The price has to decline below $90 to give some relief for the rupee. That looks less likely now. So, elevated oil prices will continue to drag the rupee lower. Published on  March 24, 2026
