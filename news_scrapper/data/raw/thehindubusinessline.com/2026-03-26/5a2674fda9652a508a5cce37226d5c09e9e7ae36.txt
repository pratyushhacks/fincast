The partnership provides access to Hellmann’s network across more than 170 countries and over 30,000 suppliers, allowing Motherson to scale globally without building logistics infrastructure from scratch Samvardhana Motherson International Ltd (SAMIL) on Wednesday said it has signed a joint venture agreement with Germany’s Hellmann Worldwide Logistics that will provide integrated supply chain solutions for the global automotive industry. The Dubai-based JV will offer end-to-end logistics solutions by combining Hellmann’s global network with Motherson’s manufacturing scale and OEM relationships, positioning the group as an integrated industrial platform. “Motherson’s Logistics Division focuses on building specialised platforms that support evolving supply chain needs,” said Arjun Kochhar, CEO of the group’s logistics business. “Together we aim to deliver more resilient, agile, and sustainable logistics networks.” The venture will complete the logistics leg of Motherson’s global DEMAL (design, engineering, manufacturing, assembly and logistics) strategy. Expanding beyond manufacturing The JV marks Motherson’s entry into third- and fourth-party logistics (3PL and 4PL), expanding its role from manufacturing to managing end-to-end supply chains. The partnership provides access to Hellmann’s network across more than 170 countries and over 30,000 suppliers, allowing Motherson to scale globally without building logistics infrastructure from scratch. This comes as automakers increasingly look to outsource supply chain management amid rising complexity driven by electrification, semiconductor dependencies, and geopolitical disruptions. Madhav Kurup, Global COO – Airfreight, Seafreight and Contract Logistics at Hellmann, said the partnership would create “a global platform that takes automotive logistics to the next level through seamless coordination and future-ready supply chain solutions.” The logistics foray opens up a new revenue stream as Motherson targets a sharp scale-up under its Vision 2030 roadmap. The group aims to nearly quadruple revenue to $108 billion by FY30, with non-automotive and service-led businesses expected to account for 25-30 per cent of the mix, up from about 5 per cent currently. “Logistics is expected to be a key pillar in that diversification, while the JV structure allows Motherson to expand in an asset-light manner — leveraging Hellmann’s infrastructure while maintaining capital discipline and supporting its goal of doubling return on capital employed (ROCE) to 40 per cent,” company official said Supply chain play The partnership is also aimed at improving supply chain stability and efficiency, particularly as disruptions — from semiconductor shortages to geopolitical tensions — continue to impact global auto production. By integrating logistics with its manufacturing operations, Motherson is looking to reduce costs linked to delays, improve visibility across supply chains, and deepen engagement with OEMs. Strategic shift The JV allows Motherson’s broader shift from a component supplier to a full-system solutions provider. While the group has historically expanded through acquisitions, the Hellmann tie-up signals a parallel strategy — using partnerships to add capabilities quickly without stretching the balance sheet. “With logistics now part of its offering, Motherson is positioning itself not just as a supplier, but as a partner in managing increasingly complex global supply chains,” industry analysts told businessline Published on  March 25, 2026
