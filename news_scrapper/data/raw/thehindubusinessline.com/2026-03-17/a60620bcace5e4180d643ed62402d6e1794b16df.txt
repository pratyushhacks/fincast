											NISSAR AHMAD Last week, Union Minister for Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying (FAH&D) and Panchayati Raj, Rajiv Ranjan Singh, announced the sanction of a ₹100-crore Integrated Aqua Park project in Anantnag district of Jammu and Kashmir during the National Conference on Cold Water Fisheries in Srinagar, in a fresh push to promote trout farming in the region. During the conference, Lieutenant Governor Manoj Sinha said the sector had also received support under the Holistic Agriculture Development Programme launched in 2022, which allocated ₹233 crore for fisheries development to promote entrepreneurship, expand trout farming and encourage value-added fish products. Fisheries expansion Central support under the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY) has also helped drive the expansion of fisheries and aquaculture in the Union Territory, with the Centre releasing more than ₹118 crore over the past five financial years. The financial assistance is aimed at strengthening fish production, infrastructure and capacity building in the sector. According to official data tabled in Parliament, Jammu and Kashmir received ₹11,850.39 lakh between 2020-21 and 2024-25. The highest release came in 2020-21 with ₹5,006.63 lakh, followed by ₹1,364.20 lakh in 2021-22, ₹2,020 lakh in 2022-23, ₹1,219.73 lakh in 2023-24 and ₹2,239.83 lakh in 2024-25. Officials said the funds have been used to strengthen aquaculture infrastructure, improve fish seed production, support fish farming units and build capacity among local farmers. The scheme also supports hatcheries, feed mills and modern aquaculture technologies aimed at enhancing productivity. The financial support has coincided with rapid growth in the fisheries sector in the Union Territory, particularly in trout farming. Jammu and Kashmir has emerged as India’s leading producer of trout among cold-water States. Surge in production Trout production in the region has nearly doubled in recent years, rising from 1,663 tonnes in 2021-22 to a record 2,650 tonnes in 2024-25. Officials said the expansion reflects a broader push to diversify the rural economy by promoting high-value sectors within agriculture and allied activities. Fisheries, particularly cold-water aquaculture, is increasingly seen as a source of employment and higher farm incomes in the Himalayan region. Experts say the combination of central funding, local policy initiatives and favourable natural conditions is positioning Jammu and Kashmir as a key hub for cold-water fisheries in India.
