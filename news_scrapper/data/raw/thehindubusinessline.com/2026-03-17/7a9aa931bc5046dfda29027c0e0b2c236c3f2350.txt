The Centre has pushed the localisation deadline for key electric bus and truck components to September 1, 2026, under the PM E-Drive scheme, a move expected to provide relief to manufacturers such as Tata Motors , Ashok Leyland , Eicher Motors, and EKA Mobility that are ramping up electric bus and commercial vehicle deployments across the country. The amendment effectively gives vehicle makers additional time to domestically manufacture traction motors used in electric buses and N2-category electric trucks, with officials acknowledging that the revised timeline factors in supply-chain disruptions flagged by OEMs, particularly shortages of rare-earth magnets used in high-efficiency electric powertrains. The Ministry of Heavy Industries notified the change through a Gazette notification dated March 13, 2026, amending the Phased Manufacturing Program (PMP) roadmap for electric trucks in the N2 and N3 commercial vehicle categories under the PM E-Drive scheme. Under the revised norms, traction motors used in these vehicles must be manufactured domestically from September 1, 2026, with key processes carried out within India. According to the notification, domestic manufacturing must include processes such as magnet fitment, rotor and stator assembly, shaft fitment, bearing integration, enclosure fitment, and connector and cable installation. The rules also mandate deeper localisation of integrated systems, including motor-transmission assemblies and traction motor controllers with inverters. For integrated traction motors used with transmissions, domestic assembly of motor-transmission systems, transmission controllers and software flashing must be undertaken locally from September 2025, followed by full domestic manufacturing from September 2026. Similarly, traction motor controllers with inverters must undergo domestic manufacturing that includes PCB assembly with electronic components and semiconductors, high-voltage connector integration, heatsink fitment, and firmware flashing. Industry executives said the revised timeline reflects persistent global supply-chain constraints, particularly the limited availability of rare-earth permanent magnets, which are essential for high-efficiency traction motors used in electric buses and heavy commercial vehicles. “High-performance traction motors rely heavily on rare-earth magnets, and global supply chains for these components remain highly concentrated. The extension gives OEMs and suppliers the necessary time to localise manufacturing without disrupting vehicle deployment,” said a senior executive at a commercial vehicle manufacturer. Officials said the extension effectively allows manufacturers to continue importing traction motor sub-assemblies temporarily while domestic production capacity is developed, ensuring that vehicle deployment programs are not disrupted. Beneficiaries across commercial EV segment The move is expected to benefit major commercial vehicle manufacturers such as Tata Motors and Ashok Leyland, both of which produce electric buses and trucks and are among the approved Champion OEMs under the government’s Auto PLI scheme aimed at boosting domestic manufacturing of advanced automotive technologies. Other commercial vehicle players such as Pinnacle Mobility Solutions and Eicher Motors are also part of the incentive programme. At the same time, dedicated electric bus manufacturers, including JBM Auto, PMI Electro Mobility, and EKA Mobility, are expected to benefit from the extension as they scale deliveries under public transport electrification programs. Industry executives said that if localisation rules had come into force earlier this year, several manufacturers could have faced production disruptions or delays in vehicle deliveries due to shortages of traction motor components. The extension is also expected to provide additional time for domestic component suppliers such as Sona BLW Precision Forgings (Sona Comstar) and Pricol to expand manufacturing capacity for traction motor systems and related EV powertrain components.
