                Quote: Maulik Patel, Head of Research, Equirus Securities views on The US FOMC

                The FOMC kept the target policy rate unchanged as we had expected. The Summary of economic projections shows that the FOMC members are expecting the PCE inflation to be higher than earlier anticipated amidst the spike in energy prices - at 2.7% for 2026 vs Dec estimate of 2.4%. Indicating expectations of a pass through to core inflation hue to higher energy prices, the members have also raised their estimates for core PCE to 2.7%. The median forecast continues to show one cut of 25 bps this year. However, the distribution of future rate expectations of the members have shifted notably. The December dot plot showed a bigger variance with expectations of multiple and bigger cuts and also a possible hike. The distribution now is spread between unchanged and lower overall cuts.

                Surprisingly, the members have raised their real GDP growth forecast for 2026 to 2.4% from 2.3% in the December meeting and 2.3% for 2027 from 2%. Hence, they are expecting no material drag on growth. Powell’s comments at the press conference signaled that this could be because of the current trend in productivity growth. However, the forecast does seem optimistic, especially if we see a moderation in AI investments alongside a drag on consumer spending with higher energy prices. Powell’s comments at the press conference highlight the uncertainty they are working with. He commented that It’s too early to say whether the effect from the war will be big or small, and too soon to know the scope and duration of the potential effects on the economy. Hence, the next SEP could look totally different if there were any big developments on the Middle East crisis.

                ICRA places InterGlobe Aviation Limited (IndiGo)’s long-term rating on Watch with Negative Implications amid West Asia conflict impact ICRA has placed the long-term rating of InterGlobe Aviation Limited (IndiGo) on ‘Watch with Negative Implications’, citing expected pressure on the airline’s operational and financial performance due to the ongoing geopolitical tensions in West Asia. The escalation of the conflict since late February 2026 has led to disruptions in international airspace, flight cancellations, route suspensions, and rerouting, particularly affecting West Asian and select long-haul routes. These disruptions are impacting capacity deployment, revenues, and operating efficiency. Adding to the pressure, Brent crude prices have surged sharply from around $70 per barrel in early February to about $102-105 per barrel, significantly increasing aviation turbine fuel (ATF) costs. Further, the Indian Rupee has depreciated by nearly 8% (around Rs. 7 per USD) in FY2026 (YTD), raising cost pressures as a substantial portion of airline expenses are dollar-linked. Fuel accounts for 35–40% of IndiGo’s cost structure, and over *60% of its expenses are linked to foreign currency, making the airline particularly vulnerable to sustained high fuel prices and currency depreciation. ICRA noted that while airlines, including IndiGo, have introduced fuel surcharges to partly offset rising costs, the ability to fully pass on these increases remains constrained by demand sensitivity. IndiGo’s international operations, which account for around 30% of total capacity, have been significantly affected, with over 500 flights cancelled between February 28 and March 3, 2026, followed by further disruptions. The airline has undertaken network realignment, including temporary suspension of services to several destinations. Additionally, the recent resignation of CEO Pieter Elbers and interim leadership transition are being monitored for potential impact on strategic execution. Despite near-term headwinds, ICRA highlighted IndiGo’s strong fundamentals, including:

                • Dominant domestic market share of ~64%
                • Strong liquidity with Rs. 36,945 crore in free cash (as of Dec 31, 2025)
                • Cost-efficient low-cost carrier model
                • Track record of managing external shocks ICRA noted that the rating outlook could stabilise if geopolitical tensions ease, leading to moderation in fuel prices and restoration of airspace access. However, a prolonged conflict and elevated cost environment could lead to further downside risks for the airline’s earnings and credit profile.
