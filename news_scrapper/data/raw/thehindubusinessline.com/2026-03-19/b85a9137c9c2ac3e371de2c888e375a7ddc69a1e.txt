Bengaluru headquartered Bosch Ltd on Tuesday said it will set up a 50:50 joint venture with Pune-based Tata Autocomp Systems Ltd to manufacture e-axles and electric traction motors in India. The proposed entity to be incorporated as a private limited company with an initial paid-up capital of ₹10 lakh and a total investment of up to ₹94 crore, as suppliers move to localise key electric vehicle drivetrain components In a regulatory filing, Bosch said its board has approved entering into a joint venture agreement to set up a new entity that will undertake the “manufacture, sale, and after-sales service of e-axles and electric traction motors and any other products”. The partnership combines Bosch’s engineering and software capabilities in electric drivetrains with Tata Autocomp’s manufacturing, sourcing, and localisation strengths, as well as its proximity to large domestic original equipment manufacturers, including within the Tata Group. The JV will focus on manufacturing, sales, and after-sales service of electric powertrain components as automakers scale up electric vehicle launches across segments. E-axles, which integrate the motor, inverter, and transmission into a single unit, are a key component of electric drivetrains and are increasingly being localised to reduce costs and improve supply chain resilience. The move comes at a time when automakers are accelerating electric vehicle launches, increasing demand for integrated electric powertrain systems. E-axles, which combine the motor, inverter, and transmission into a single unit and are emerging as a core component in EV architectures, helping simplify vehicle design and improve efficiency. Industry executives said localisation of such systems will be critical as automakers look to reduce costs and improve supply chain resilience, particularly in high-volume segments such as compact passenger vehicles and light commercial vehicles. Bosch, which reported India revenues of ₹18,087 crore in FY25, said it will contribute engineering and development expertise, while Tata Autocomp will support operations, procurement and administration. The companies will enter a segment that is seeing increasing competition, with players such as Sona Comstar, Dana and BorgWarner already building capabilities in electric drivetrains and e-axle systems. The joint venture will be incorporated as a private limited company, with both partners holding equal stakes. with the agreement is expected to be executed on March 23, 2026, subject to regulatory approvals and will be governed by a six-member board with equal representation from both partners,  subject to a five-year lock-in period on share transfers.
