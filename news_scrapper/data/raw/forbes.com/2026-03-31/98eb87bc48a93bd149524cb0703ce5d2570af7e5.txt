(Photo by LLUIS GENE / AFP) (Photo by LLUIS GENE/AFP via Getty Images) AFP via Getty Images When Dave Blundin , co-founder of Link Ventures and a managing partner overseeing early-stage AI companies, flagged the Situational Awareness fund's 13F filings on X earlier this month, the observation was pointed: W-2 income is going to get pummeled over the next three years, but the tailwinds in equities are like nothing you have ever seen. Blundin, who co-founded 23 companies , chairs EverQuote (NASDAQ: EVER), and founded Vestmark, which manages over $2 trillion in U.S. assets, is not a person who overstates things. The fund he was pointing to belongs to Leopold Aschenbrenner , a former OpenAI researcher fired from the company's superalignment team in 2024. Rather than retreating, Aschenbrenner launched Situational Awareness LP, a San Francisco-based hedge fund built directly on the thesis of his 165-page 2024 essay by the same name, which argued that AGI was coming faster than governments and investors understood, and that the U.S. needed to act accordingly. From Essay to $5.5 Billion in Equity Exposure The fund started with anchor commitments from Patrick and John Collison, founders of Stripe, along with Nat Friedman and Daniel Gross. According to Fortune , Situational Awareness now reports roughly $5.5 billion in U.S. equity exposure across approximately 30 holdings. That figure represents its 13F-disclosed long position book; actual AUM, per the fund's Form ADV, is reported at $383 million, as 13F filings capture gross long exposure including leverage, not total assets under management. The growth is still notable. Per CoinTelegraph , the fund's 13F-reported U.S. equity and options portfolio grew from approximately $254 million in Q4 2024 to over $5.5 billion by Q4 2025. Aschenbrenner has invested nearly all of his own net worth in the fund. The Portfolio: Electricity and Compute, Not Models The Q4 2025 13F holdings, circulated widely on X and sourced in the image below, show eight top positions totaling just over $3.2 billion in reported value. Bloom Energy Corp leads at $875.5 million (22% allocation, 10.1 million shares), followed by Lumentum Holdings at $478.6 million (12%), CoreWeave at $436.7 million (11%), and Core Scientific at $418.7 million (11%). IREN Limited, Applied Digital Corp, SanDisk Corp, and Cipher Mining round out the top eight, with allocations ranging from 4% to 8%. The logic is explicit. Rather than owning the companies building AI models, Aschenbrenner is owning the infrastructure those models require. As Fortune reported , the portfolio suggests a clear thesis: the most valuable assets in the AI era may not be algorithms, but electricity and computing power. Bloom Energy supplies fuel-cell power. CoreWeave provides AI cloud infrastructure. Core Scientific, IREN, Applied Digital, and Cipher Mining are large-scale compute operators that converted Bitcoin mining capacity into AI hosting facilities. That last category is particularly instructive. Per CoinTelegraph , Core Scientific has signed 12-year high-performance computing hosting contracts with CoreWeave. After Bitcoin's last halving squeezed block rewards, miners with power-dense facilities found a second market: renting megawatts and rack space to AI companies that cannot build data centers fast enough. What VCs Should Take From This The conventional VC playbook for AI exposure runs through foundation model companies, application-layer startups, and developer tooling. Situational Awareness offers a different frame: the innermost loop of AI is not the software, it is the physical constraint. Whoever controls the electrons and the GPUs controls the rate of progress. Blundin's point about W-2 income carries a separate implication for venture capital. As AI systems absorb more cognitive labor, the expected return to human capital in knowledge work compresses. The expected return to capital ownership, specifically to the infrastructure enabling that displacement, expands in the opposite direction. This is not a novel thesis in macroeconomics, but 13F filings give it a concrete, publicly auditable expression. For VCs, the relevance is structural. Most early-stage funds cannot own Bloom Energy or CoreWeave directly at scale. But they can use the Situational Awareness portfolio as a map: the companies being seeded today that will eventually become suppliers to, customers of, or competitors with that infrastructure layer are where the next decade of venture alpha lives. Aschenbrenner’s essay opened with a line that summarized his view: you can see the future first in San Francisco. His 13F filings suggest he is already positioned there.
