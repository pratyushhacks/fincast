The Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA) is an annual increase in benefits such as Social Security to help keep pace with inflation. It is designed to ensure that individuals—especially retirees—can maintain their purchasing power as the cost of everyday goods and services rises. COLA is calculated based on changes in inflation, typically measured using a government price index. When inflation rises, benefits are adjusted upward; if inflation remains low, the increase may be smaller or even unchanged. For millions of people who rely on fixed incomes, COLA plays a crucial role in maintaining financial stability. Without these adjustments, rising costs for essentials like food, housing and healthcare could reduce their standard of living over time. In the United States, the COLA for Social Security is usually announced in October each year and takes effect the following January. The adjustment reflects inflation trends recorded earlier in the year.
